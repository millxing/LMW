Protecting the grove

Beech Leaf Disease

What Beech Leaf Disease is, how it affects the trees of Longwood Mall, and what is being done to protect them.

What it is

A disease of beech trees caused by a microscopic nematode that feeds inside the leaves. It was first found in Ohio in 2012 and reached Massachusetts in 2020.

Status at Longwood Mall

As of June 2026, Brookline's Tree Warden reports little visible evidence of the disease in the grove. Some older trees show age-related decline.

What is being done

Foliar spray treatments each summer since 2022, close monitoring, a planned trial of the injectable Arbortect 20-S, and new plantings.

How you can help

Support the treatment and planting fund, and help keep people off trunks and branches, which stresses already-vulnerable trees.

About the disease

Beech Leaf Disease (BLD) is caused by a microscopic roundworm, the nematode Litylenchus crenatae mccannii, that lives and feeds inside beech leaves. The first sign is dark green banding between the leaf veins, usually on lower branches. Over several years, leaves shrink, curl, and drop early, buds fail, and the canopy thins.

Young trees can die within a few years. Large, mature trees like the beeches of Longwood Mall decline more slowly, which gives time to treat them. The disease affects both the native American beech and the European beech planted here.

BLD was first found in Lake County, Ohio, in 2012 and has spread across the Northeast, reaching Massachusetts in 2020. Birds, insects, and people may all help move the nematodes from tree to tree.

There is no cure, but treatment can hold the disease back. At Longwood Mall, foliar spray treatments every summer since 2022 appear to be keeping it in check.
